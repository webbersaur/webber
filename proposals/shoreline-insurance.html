Section 01

Why Now

Two dates this fall change how Scope of Appointment forms work, and they land back to back. Together they turn a paper SOA process from a mild inconvenience into a direct constraint on how fast you can enroll someone.

October 1, 2026: the 48-hour waiting period goes away

Rule Change

CMS has finalized the elimination of the fixed 48-hour waiting period between a completed Scope of Appointment and the personal marketing appointment. Beginning October 1 you can collect the SOA and hold the appointment the same day, provided the form is complete before any plan-specific discussion starts. The SOA itself is still required. What disappears is the mandatory wait.

October 15, 2026: Annual Enrollment Period opens

Peak Season

AEP runs October 15 through December 7, your highest-volume stretch of the year. The rule change arrives exactly two weeks before it starts.

Why the combination matters

Right now a mailed or faxed SOA costs you very little, because you were required to wait 48 hours regardless. The paperwork delay hides inside the regulatory delay. On October 1 the regulatory delay disappears and the paperwork delay becomes the only thing left standing between a ready prospect and a signed enrollment.

An agent who can put a signed SOA on a client's phone in two minutes can keep the conversation going and close it the same day. An agent still mailing forms is asking a motivated prospect to wait several days and then remember to come back. During AEP, when that prospect is also being called by every competitor in the state, that gap is where business goes.

The build takes 14 days. Starting now puts you live in early September, giving you a full month to get comfortable with the workflow before the rule change and AEP arrive together.

Section 02

The Current Process

As a Medicare specialist you handle protected health information every day. Printing, mailing, faxing, and emailing forms creates both compliance exposure and friction that costs you time and enrollments.

PHI Moving Through Unsecured Channels

Critical

Agents handling protected health information on behalf of Medicare carriers operate under business associate obligations that flow down through carrier and FMO agreements. Unencrypted email attachments, faxes, and paper forms in transit sit outside those obligations. The practical exposure is not an abstract federal penalty, it is a breach that runs back through your carrier contracts and puts your appointments at risk.

No Tamper-Evident Audit Trail

Compliance Gap

A wet signature on a scanned page does not establish who signed, when they signed, or from where. If a carrier audits an SOA or a beneficiary disputes what was authorized, you need timestamped, tamper-evident proof. Filed paper and emailed PDFs do not provide it.

Turnaround Measured in Days

High Impact

Mail out, wait, hope it comes back, follow up by phone, re-send the ones that got lost. Every step is manual and every step is a place the process stalls. During AEP that turnaround directly limits how many appointments you can run.

Friction for the Client

Medium

Asking someone to print, sign, scan, and email a form back assumes they own a printer and a scanner and know how to use both. Many do not. The result is a phone call to walk them through it, or a drive to their kitchen table for a signature you could have collected in two minutes.
