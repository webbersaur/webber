The audit

Rudy's has the history. The website isn't telling Google, or your guests.

People have been going to Rudy's for decades, first on Elm Street and now at Chapel and Howe. But someone who finds you on Google today lands on a St. Patrick's Day menu, and other pages still have leftover template text, including a burger joint in Brooklyn and a phone number that doesn't work. We went through the site page by page, from the outside. Here's what we found, in plain terms. Every item is covered in the price above.

Fix first What a guest sees, and what Google reads

  • Home page

    Your home page is the St. Patrick's Day parade special.

    rudysnewhaven.com opens on a menu with a corned beef sandwich, pastrami and $6 Bud Light. It doesn't show your address, hours or phone number. The real home page, with all of that, sits at a separate address (/welcome) that Google treats as a different page.

  • Template text

    A page on your site says "BEST BURGER IN BROOKLYN."

    It's leftover sample text from the Squarespace template ("Locally Sourced × Crafted with Love"), still live at rudysnewhaven.com/home where Google can find it.

  • Template text

    A second "Menu" page lists dishes Rudy's doesn't serve.

    /menu mixes the St. Patrick's specials with template dishes like Lobster Bolognese ($28), Dry-Aged Rib-Eye ($36), Braised Lamb and Salmon Steak. Your real menu, with the frites, wings and Belgian beers, is at /menu-2.

  • Wrong number

    The Contact page lists the phone number as (512) 555-0110.

    That's a made-up Austin, Texas number from the template. Your real number, (203) 865-1242, is only on the welcome page. Google pays close attention to whether your phone number matches everywhere.

  • Mismatch

    The site shows two different sets of hours.

    One page says Sunday to Thursday, 11:30am to 1am. The Contact page says Monday to Thursday 11:30am to 1am and Sunday 10:30am to 1am. Guests and Google can't tell which is right.

  • Broken

    The EMAIL link at the bottom of every page doesn't work.

    The address got stray quote marks around it in the setup, so many phones and email apps can't open it.

Getting found on Google

  • Missing

    No page has a description for Google.

    That's the two-line summary under your name in search results. Without one, Google picks random text from the page, which right now could be a sandwich price.

  • Weak

    Page titles are just "Rudy's," "Home," "Menu."

    None of them say New Haven, bar, Belgian frites or beer, which are the words people actually search. A title like "Rudy's | Belgian Frites & Beer on Chapel St, New Haven" works much harder.

  • Missing

    No main headline on any real page.

    Google uses each page's main heading to understand what the page is about. The only two the site has are "White Claw Party" and "BEST BURGER IN BROOKLYN."

  • Missing

    Google gets almost no facts about the business.

    Behind the scenes, the site only tells Google it's "a website called Rudy's." It should say you're a bar and restaurant at 1227 Chapel Street, with your hours, phone, menu and online ordering link. That's what helps you show up in maps and "near me" searches.

  • Hidden

    The Grubhub order button is buried.

    It's only on the menu page. It isn't in the main menu or on the home page, where people ready to order would look.

Content and leftovers

  • Thin

    The site never tells the Rudy's story.

    The home page has about 70 words. There's nothing about the Elm Street years, the move to Chapel and Howe, the memorabilia or the frites. That history is the biggest thing that sets you apart, and it's exactly what Google and newcomers respond to.

  • Outdated

    A "White Claw Party" from February 2020 is still listed.

    It's the only event on the Events page, and it's still in the list of pages the site hands to Google.

  • Leftovers

    Three links on the welcome page just say "New Page."

    Blank placeholder pages make the site look unfinished.

  • Missing

    Photos are labeled with camera file names.

    Descriptions like "IMG_0022.JPG" tell Google nothing. "Belgian frites at Rudy's New Haven" does.

  • Weak

    Shared links look bare.

    When someone texts or posts a link to the site, the preview just says "Rudy's," with no description.
