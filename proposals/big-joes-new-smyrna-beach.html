Google thinks you're only a sub shop

Your listing is set up as Sandwich shop, Hamburger restaurant, Restaurant and Salad shop. There's no Breakfast category, so Google has no reason to show you when people search for breakfast.

Your website link goes to Facebook

Both the website button and the menu button on your Google listing open Facebook. Google can't read a menu or your breakfast hours there.

Breakfast details only live in Facebook posts

The 6 to 11 hours, the early bird specials, the homemade biscuits and sausage gravy: all of it is in posts that scroll away within days.

Why breakfast is winnable

  • Reviews

    Your reviews already match the leaders.

    4.7 stars from 1,161 reviews is as strong as anyone in the breakfast top 3. Most new breakfast spots spend years building that. You start with it.

  • Story

    The Pappas crew is a story people want to share.

    Pappas fed New Smyrna Beach for over 50 years before the fire in March 2024. Folks who miss that breakfast are searching for it. A page that says "the Pappas breakfast crew is cooking at Big Joe's" gives them, and Google, something to find.

  • Buzz

    Guests are already talking about it.

    Recent reviews give a shout-out to "the breakfast crew," and breakfast posts and videos are getting attention on Facebook. Google listens to what reviews say, so more breakfast reviews will help.

  • Timing

    Breakfast is only about two months old.

    Now is the moment to put it on Google. Every week it's missing, those searches go to the places down the street.

  • Next

    Thinking about dinner? Same playbook.

    When you add dinner, it gets its own menu page on the site, dinner hours and menu items on your Google listing, and a launch post. The website is built so adding it takes days, not a new project. With a Facebook page, dinner would have the same problem breakfast has today.
