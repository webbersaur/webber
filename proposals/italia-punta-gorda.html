Website and search audit

Italia has the story, the kitchen and the location. The website isn't telling Google.

Italia raises its own Red Angus for the meatballs and bolognese, cooks with local clams and Worden Farms produce, and sits steps from the harbor in downtown Punta Gorda. But when Google reads the site, it finds a menu that's only a picture, photos so large the home page takes over half a minute to load on a phone, and pages that haven't changed since 2019. We went through the site page by page, from the outside. Here's what we found, in plain terms.

Fix first What a guest sees, and what Google reads

  • Menu

    Your menu is a picture, so Google can't read any of it.

    The Menu page shows one small image of the December 2024 menu. Google doesn't read text inside photos, so none of your dishes help you show up for searches like "calamari Punta Gorda," "pizza downtown Punta Gorda" or "Italian restaurant near me." On a phone the image is also hard to read. Putting the menu on the page as regular text fixes both.

  • Speed

    The home page takes about 35 seconds to finish loading on a phone.

    Two photos are straight off the camera: one is 5.8 MB and the other 2.1 MB, about 9 MB in total. Google's own speed test scores the home page 64 out of 100 on mobile. Most people leave long before the picture appears. Resizing those photos fixes most of this in an afternoon.

  • Hours

    Your hours don't match from one listing to the next.

    The website says Tuesday to Saturday, closed Monday and Sunday. A Roadtrippers listing says Monday to Saturday, 4:30 to 8:30, and MapQuest shows Italia as closed. Google compares these sources, and a guest who sees "closed" just goes somewhere else.

  • Name

    The restaurant goes by several names online.

    The home page alone says "Italia," "Italia at River City" and "Italia River City Grill," and the contact email is the River City Grill address. Google uses a consistent name to connect your website, your map listing and your reviews. Pick one and use it everywhere.

Getting found on Google

  • Missing

    Google gets almost no facts about the restaurant.

    Behind the scenes, the site only tells Google it's "a web page." It should say you're an Italian restaurant at 127 W. Marion Ave, with your hours, phone, menu, price range and reservations. That's what helps you show up in maps and "near me" searches.

  • Blind spot

    The site isn't counting visitors.

    There's no Google Analytics, so there's no way to see how many people visit, where they come from, or whether they look at the menu.

  • Missing

    Half the pages have no description for Google.

    That's the two-line summary under your name in search results. Chef's Corner, Private Dining and the news post don't have one, so Google picks whatever text it finds.

  • Missing

    None of the photos are described.

    Google can't see pictures. A description like "Bolognese with house-raised Red Angus at Italia, Punta Gorda" tells it what's on the plate. Right now every photo is blank to Google.

  • Typo

    The About page title says "Flordia."

    It shows up in Google search results and in browser tabs.

Content and leftovers

  • Buried

    Your best story is hidden on the About page.

    Your own Red Angus from Onion River Ranch, local clams, Worden Farms and Aloe Organics produce, Harrington heirloom tomatoes, San Marzanos, and house-infused oils you sell at the table. Very few restaurants can say that. It belongs on the home page, where guests and Google see it first.

  • Thin

    Private dining gets two sentences.

    A room for 30 is a real reason to book with Italia, and people search for "private dining Punta Gorda," rehearsal dinners and birthday parties. The page has no headline and no photos of the room.

  • Outdated

    The site still says 2019.

    The footer reads "© 2019," the only news post is "New Website Launched!" from February 2019, and its share buttons include Google+, which shut down that year.

  • Reviews

    The home page shows two short quotes instead of your real reviews.

    Guests online praise the calamari, the pizza and the service. Showing those real Google reviews builds much more trust.

  • Phones

    Guests can't zoom in on phones.

    The site blocks pinch-to-zoom, which makes small text and the menu picture even harder to read.

What's working

  • Strength

    The site is secure and the address works every way.

    Every version of italiapg.com forwards cleanly to the secure https://www. version, and the server answers quickly.

  • Strength

    The basics for Google are in place.

    The site has a sitemap, nothing is blocked from Google, and the main pages have sensible titles like "Italia: Classic Italian Restaurant - Punta Gorda, Florida."

  • Strength

    The phone number is tap-to-call.

    Guests on phones can call for a reservation or carry-out in one tap.

  • Strength

    You have more to talk about than almost anyone in town.

    A CIA-trained chef-owner, a corporate chef with decades on the line, your own cattle and local farm partners. The material is there. Google just can't see it yet.

Where we'd start

  1. Confirm your real hours and one official name, then make the website, Google and the outside listings match.
  2. Put the menu on the site as text that's easy to update, and keep a picture or PDF as a download.
  3. Resize the home page photos so the site loads in a couple of seconds on a phone.
  4. Move the ranch-to-table story to the home page with a clear headline: Italian restaurant in downtown Punta Gorda.
  5. Add the behind-the-scenes restaurant details Google uses for maps and "near me" searches.
  6. Give private dining a real page with photos of the room and a way to ask about dates.
  7. Show your real Google reviews, describe every photo, and fix the 2019 leftovers and the "Flordia" typo.
  8. Install Google Analytics so you can see your traffic.

This review covers what anyone can see from the outside. We haven't looked at your Google Business Profile, your WordPress account or your visitor numbers. Those would come next.
