The Review Engine

You know you should ask every customer for a Google review. You don't. Almost nobody does. So we stopped asking you to.

See How It Works Talk to Us

Everyone Knows Reviews Matter. Almost Nobody Asks.

When somebody searches for what you do, Google shows a map with three businesses on it. Getting into that map is worth more than any other thing on your website, and your star rating and review count are among the strongest signals deciding who lands there.

Every owner already knows this. And still the reviews do not come, because the moment a job ends you are already loading the truck for the next one. Asking means stopping, remembering, digging up an email address, and finding the words.

Any system that needs you to start something after a job is finished will be dead by week three. Reminder cards, checklists, a note on the invoice, the best intentions in the world. We have watched it happen over and over.

So we stopped trying to build a better reminder. The reminder was never the problem.

Don't Add a Step. Attach to One That Already Happens.

You already email your customer something when the work is done. An invoice, a quote, a receipt, a confirmation. That email already has their name on it and their address in it.

So put one more address in the BCC line. That is the entire new habit.

Nothing to log into. Nothing to remember later. Nothing to type twice. Your customer never sees it, because the BCC line is invisible to them by design. From there, the engine takes over.

And because it is you who adds that address, you decide who gets asked. The job that went sideways never gets BCC'd at all.

You are the filter. The engine only ever asks the customers you already know were happy.

Every one carries a quiet "tell us privately instead" link that goes straight to you, not to Google. An unhappy customer gets a way to be heard without it becoming a public star - and the moment anyone clicks, unsubscribes, or leaves a review, the rest of the sequence stops for good.

For the ones who want to help but freeze at the blank box

Most people who never leave a review are not unwilling. They open the box, do not know what to write, and close it. So right under the button, the email offers a few gentle prompts - things they might mention, in their own words:

Right under the button, in the email

Not sure what to write? You could mention:

• Were we on time and easy to work with?
• How did the finished work turn out?
• Would you use us again, or recommend us to a neighbor?

A sentence or two in your own words is all it takes.

Note what this is not: a canned review you paste. Google filters identical, templated review text - so a script handed to every customer gets the very reviews you generated quietly removed. These are prompts, not words in their mouth. Everyone still writes their own, and everyone still goes to the same Google form.

Volume Is the Only Thing That Moves a Rating

Businesses lose the map spot on their star rating more often than on anything else. And a rating is an average, which means it is stubborn: you cannot argue it upward, you can only outvote it. Every genuinely happy customer who never got asked is a vote you left on the table.

22 reviews at 4.1 stars = 4.10 average + 20 new 5-star reviews = 4.53 average

Illustrative, not a promise

That gap is often the whole difference between showing up in the map and being invisible in it. There is no clever way to get there. You get there by asking every single customer, every single time, which is exactly the thing no human being reliably does by hand, and exactly the thing a machine is good at.

The other half is recency. A steady trickle of new reviews reads as a business that is busy right now. Twenty reviews from three years ago read as a business that used to be.

It Does Not Work for Everybody

We would rather tell you it is the wrong fit than sell it to you twice.

Built for this

  • Trades and home services: plumbing, HVAC, roofing, siding, paving, landscaping, remodeling
  • Practices that book appointments: dental, medical, physical therapy, legal, insurance
  • Real estate agents: a review after every closing is what wins the next listing
  • Anyone who finishes a job and emails a bill

Wrong tool

  • Walk-in restaurants and retail. You never captured a name or an email, so there is nothing to work with. You want a QR code on the table instead, and we will build you one.
  • Businesses selling nationally to other businesses, where a local map listing is not how anyone finds you.
